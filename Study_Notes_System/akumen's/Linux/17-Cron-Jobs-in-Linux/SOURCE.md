Original supplied material for Topic 17 (Cron Jobs in Linux):

Cron is a time-based job scheduler in Unix-like operating systems. Users use cron to schedule jobs (commands or shell scripts) to run periodically at fixed times, dates, or intervals.

Key concepts covered:
- Cron fundamentals and crontab operations
- 5-field time syntax: Minute, Hour, Day of Month, Month, Day of Week
- Wildcards, steps, ranges, and lists
- Cron shortcuts (@reboot, @daily, @hourly, @weekly, @monthly, @yearly)
- Redirection (stdout, stderr, 2>&1, /dev/null)
- Environment variables and PATH limitations in cron
- System-wide cron configuration (/etc/crontab, /etc/cron.d, /etc/cron.daily, etc.)
- Security access control (/etc/cron.allow, /etc/cron.deny, least privilege)
- Cron alternatives (anacron, systemd timers, Kubernetes CronJobs)
- Troubleshooting, hands-on labs, interview QA, and MCQs
